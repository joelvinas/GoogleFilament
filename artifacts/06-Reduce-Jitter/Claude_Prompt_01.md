> **Context:** Two-finger pan and pinch-zoom both work correctly now (fixed in a prior branch, merged). One remaining issue: two-finger pan gets visibly jittery/jerky specifically when the two touch points are close together (e.g., thumb and index finger nearly touching). It smooths out once fingers are roughly 0.5–1cm apart. Zoom is unaffected at any finger separation. One-finger orbit is unaffected.
>
> **Working theory (unconfirmed — diagnose before fixing):** touchscreens have real precision limits disambiguating two contact points when they're close together, and that shows up as correlated noise — both reported points wobbling together, frame to frame, even when the actual fingers are moving smoothly. Pan's centroid is a plain average of the two raw points with no smoothing, so it would inherit that shared jitter at full strength. Zoom's span comes from Android's `ScaleGestureDetector.scaleFactor`, which already does internal smoothing on span calculations — and since the noise is correlated (both points moving together), computing *distance* between them would cancel out a lot of it before Android's own smoothing even applies. That would explain why zoom stays clean and pan doesn't, and why it scales with finger separation specifically.
>
> **Step 1 — confirm before changing anything.** Add temporary logging of the raw per-pointer (x, y) coordinates from the `MotionEvent` during a close-fingers pan, alongside the computed centroid each frame. Capture a real on-device log and check: is the jitter visibly present in the *raw* pointer data itself, or does it only appear after our centroid math? This distinguishes "sensor-level noise we need to filter" from "something in our processing is amplifying it" — don't assume, check.
>
> **Step 2 — if confirmed as raw input noise, apply smoothing to the centroid before it drives `grabUpdate`.** Standard approach: an exponential moving average (EMA) on the centroid — `smoothedCentroid = smoothedCentroid + alpha * (rawCentroid - smoothedCentroid)` — reset whenever a new two-finger gesture starts (don't carry smoothing state across gestures). This is a tuning problem, not a single correct value: too little smoothing won't remove the jitter, too much makes pan feel laggy/rubber-bandy. Implement `alpha` as an easily-adjustable constant (not buried in logic), and provide it at 2–3 candidate strengths (e.g., a lighter and heavier smoothing option) so it can actually be felt out on-device rather than guessed once and shipped.
>
> **Do not apply any smoothing to zoom's `scroll()` path** — it isn't exhibiting the problem, and touching it risks introducing lag into something that already works well.
